433 MHz Receiver Wiring

Eight wires, straight from the ESP32 to the CC1101 radio. No breadboard rails needed. Tick each wire off as you go. Your ticks are saved in this browser.

ESP32-WROOM-32 dev board DWEII CC1101 module, 433 MHz, with SMA antenna 8 jumper wires

3.3 V only. The CC1101 is not 5 V tolerant. VCC goes to the ESP32's 3V3 pin, never VIN or 5V. Screw the antenna on before you power it up. The board's pin order varies between batches, so go by the labels printed next to each pin, not by position.

First power-up: what you should see

  1. The ESP32's blue LED blinks twice: the radio started.
  2. The serial monitor prints WiFi connected. Open http://rf433.local and the ESP32's address, then Listening on 433.92 MHz.
  3. Open http://rf433.local on your phone or computer (or the number address if that doesn't load; Android phones often need the number). Your weather station should show up under Weather and sensors within a minute or two.
  4. Walk past the driveway sensor. It shows up under Alarms and remotes, the page flashes a red ALERT banner, and the blue LED stays on for 30 seconds.
  5. Neighbors' sensors often show up too. That's normal at 433 MHz. Give yours names so you can tell them apart (see below).

If nothing shows up

  • Check the module says 433 on the board or its bag. The 315, 868 and 915 MHz versions look the same but hear 433 MHz poorly.
  • Check GDO0 goes to D13. It's the wire that carries the radio signal, and it's the easiest one to put on the wrong side of the board.
  • Keep the antenna upright and a foot or so from the ESP32 and USB power supply.
  • If the driveway alarm still never appears, its code may be one rtl_433 doesn't know yet. Tell me the brand and model and I'll add it.

Naming your devices

Each device on the web page has a gray ID under its name, like Generic-Remote-12345. Add it to the NAMES list near the top of src/main.cpp, or just send me the IDs and I'll do it:

{"Generic-Remote-12345", "Driveway Alarm"},
{"Acurite-5n1-1234-A",   "Weather Station"},

Uploading

cd ~/esp32_rf433
~/.platformio/penv/bin/pio run -t upload -t monitor

It uses the same Wi-Fi login as your lightning detector. Home Assistant picks up each device automatically once MQTT is filled in src/secrets.h.
